## Tell us about a project or piece of work you’re especially proud of – from any point in your career. *
### What made it great? What was your role, and what parts were you personally responsible for? We’d love to hear about any architectural or technical decisions, trade-offs, and challenges you had to navigate along the way.
One project I’m especially proud of is Manaburn, a free-to-play multiplayer mobile role-playing game. I was responsible for architecting, developing, and deploying a scalable backend platform to serve AI-generated themed art and storylines
 (game assets).  Utilizing Python and FastAPI, I integrated the system with AWS services such as EC2, Lambda, S3, CloudFront, SES, SNS, API Gateway, and DynamoDB, ensuring a robust and cost-effective architecture. 

I had not implemented a production quality AWS deploy prior to this, but surprised myself when instincts from past experiences did kick in to check, secure, validate, test before rolling out to production.  It is exhausting to do it without a 
full team but exceedingly rewarding.

My efforts optimized cloud infrastructure costs by 100% and reduced latency by 30%, enabling seamless performance for users.

Key Contributions:
Scalable Backend Architecture: I designed and implemented a backend platform to generate and serve game assets, including custom AI-generated art and storylines. I leveraged AWS Lambda for cost efficiency and scalability, with integrations 
across the AWS ecosystem.
Custom AI Solutions: I developed and deployed a custom Stable Diffusion model as an API service for generating art and storylines, focusing on maintaining thematic consistency-a key challenge in AI-generated art.
Cost and Performance Optimization: By implementing caching strategies, prompt engineering, and OpenAI API cost reduction techniques, I significantly reduced ongoing expenses. I also fine-tuned AWS services to improve performance while keeping 
costs minimal.
Marketing Tool Development: As part of the Google Accelerator program, I created a frontend marketing tool using TypeScript, React, and Vue, which successfully attracted 15% more users during the pilot phase. The tool was deployed via 
AWS Amplify with automated CI/CD pipelines.
Challenges and Solutions:
One of the key challenges was ensuring data integrity and security while maintaining cost efficiency. I used Pydantic for data validation and implemented a schema to prevent corruption during data transit. Initially, the MVP was deployed 
on AWS EC2 using Docker for rapid prototyping, but I later migrated to AWS Lambda for scalability and better resource utilization. I also addressed concerns like image watermarking and API optimization to improve the user experience.

Additionally, I set up processes and tools to enable the creative team to generate assets iteratively without requiring my direct involvement. This included abstracting configurations and creating utilities for prompt engineering.

Reflections:
Manaburn was an incredible learning experience, allowing me to collaborate with a creative and passionate team. I gained valuable insights into the intersection of AI, gaming, and scalable architecture. While I ultimately stepped away from the project after being offered a co-founder role, I’m proud of the technical foundation I built and the processes I implemented to ensure the platform’s long-term success.
This project reignited my passion for backend development and research, and it reminded me of the importance of balancing rapid prototyping with scalable, maintainable solutions. It also underscored the value of thorough documentation for future developers and stakeholders-a practice I carried forward from my previous work in banking.  
I also saw the importance of setting up development flows and infrastructure in which a dev team works on, how much friction it reduces when building a production worthy product from ground up, the extent of which the fields of devops, platform/production engineering, front end dev has grown in the past years.

## What’s a controversial or unpopular opinion you hold about software engineering, and why? *
### This could be about tools, practices, philosophies - anything where your take might challenge conventional wisdom. We’re interested in how you think, not whether we agree.
That the best end product is a compromise between what you can afford to spend in time and cost (maintenance or outlay), and your wish list.  I know many companies, startups, simply want the latest tech and the best.  
With backend work in particular, even most of frontend work, no one can tell the difference between a 1 mil backend and 1k backend unless the tech (code, thus algorithms, are not patentable) is proprietary, it just has to work well 
(in all scenarios - various failure scenarios on various devices, responsiveness, security, scalability).  The thing everyone does not seem to take to heart, is context.  What may work well for one, may not work well for another, even through 
rigorous testing.  At Wells Fargo, we do intensive testing (our reputation and credibility depends on it), but the production issue backlogs are long.  The code is simple, so we can onboard easily, maintain it easily, secure it, it is readable, 
it goes through at least 10 rounds of different testing before it sees the light of day.  The requirements take extensive thought to suss out so it is clear, consistent with the platform and intuitive, including user flows.  We still miss user 
flow scenarios consistently because thw rold changes, humans change.  I have spent a few weeks chasing down a webpage failure in our applictaion process for one of our deposit products (I would have rolled my eyes when I graudtaed college at 
this seemingly rudimentary issue) but as a senior developer, I was chasing out possible phishing, DoS attacks because we found some weird hidden ascii characters in the forms (yes, our data validation caught that as it should).  Turns out, 
there was an influx of foreign students opening up bank accounts, the translators on browsers filled the forms out and left some ascii characters in their conversions.
